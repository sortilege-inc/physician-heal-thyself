---
title: Letters
part: Wednesday, 9 June 2021
---

The letters came in Baruch Espinosa's own hand, one to each of them, and none of them said the
same thing.

To Claire:

> Our esteemed Warlock
>
> I believe it will be to your delight that I introduce my latest acquisitions; the seventh, and the ninth that precedes the eighth. Do come and witness my latest acquisition, and sharpen your knives.

To Calliope:

> My intemperate Promethea,
>
> These nights see us poised upon a knife's edge. Our city lacks vision, and is fraught with the squabbles of the leaderless. This detente cannot hold, and when the opportunity arises, power must be grasped, swiftly and without remorse. I care not the face of a new order, for I have seen the rise and fall of Anarch, Camarilla, and Sabbat, each in turn. Use the tool you find most pliant.
>
> For now, I caution patience. Be ye the virgin with oil for the night, vigilant to seize upon the bridegroom, and to take his place to consummate influence.
>
> Watchfully yours, Baruch.

To Richart:

> My dear ancilla
>
> In these modern nights, one must assume all discrete communications lack discretion, and that any apparent cryptogram must elicit a remedy, even years hence. As such, and having learned from the failures of past systems, I have found myself enamored with the arts and sciences of steganography. When one reads the surface and is contented, the esoteric meanings may be relayed without danger or harm.
>
> To this end, may I advise that you watch the fortunes of the Dynamo ARSQ this season, in our city's Chronicle-Telegraph.

To Kamal:

> To our austere Assamite,
>
> A Spaniard ductus walks carelessly through these streets. It would be tragic if the steel trap of a kind of justice were to snap upon him, and he be waylaid.
>
> His blood is old and strong and, while I am no sommelier, carries certain notes that you may be able to appreciate.
>
> Yours, Baruch Espinosa

To Nico:

> An absent-minded lech such as yourself may notice that histories, both personal and societal, suffer themes and variations. The Levant harkens, leaving a vacuum to be abhorred à la débandade. Surely an oracle such as yourself sees the opportunity in this. It would please me greatly to consult.
>
> Yours truly, Baruch

---

They fed first, those who could.

Nico was finishing a bender with somebody. By the end of it they were both well drunk, and they
hooked up, and he fed, and he came on to Baruch's a little buzzed. Claire took a blood bag at the
hospital, which was the kind of thing a night nurse could do. Richart fed at the hospital too, on
someone asleep. Calliope slipped in somewhere on her way and took a quick nip.

Kamal could not feed on mortals, and he was very hungry. He meant to tough it out. He arrived on
time and composed, and there was nothing to say how hungry he was, except that his stigmata had
opened and his cuffs were wet.

---

It was about one in the morning. Baruch's haven lay down an old tunnel, and the entrance was
usually well hidden.

Richart and Claire arrived at about the same time. Richart saw it at once: the entrance was more
obvious than it should have been. Nobody had taken the usual care to hide it.

"Bonsoir, Claire."

"Have you been here long?"

"No, I just got off my shift, so I figured I'd get here with some time to spare to make sure I'm
not late."

Richart went on in to enjoy some hospitality while they waited. Claire followed.

Kamal came close behind them. Something was off, and he could not tell whether it was only that
others had got there first. He went in alone.

Before they had even reached the haven proper, Richart reached past his ordinary senses into the
dark ahead, and new death met him there. He could not tell whose.

"Well, I believe that up ahead we may find something rather messy. Are you up for it?"

"I was told to sharpen my knives before coming over," said Claire, "so maybe that's to be
expected."

Richart took a small handgun out of his coat and fitted a silencer to it. He texted the coterie's
group chat: something was not right at Baruch's; proceed with caution. Calliope had given the
rest of them burner phones. Claire still carried a pager. Richart had a smartphone and did not care
who knew it.

Kamal came up behind them without a sound. The buzz of a phone was the only sign he was there.

---

Nico had got there early and hidden outside, to jump out at Calliope when she came.

Calliope came late, adjusting her jacket, reading Richart's message. She did not look up.

"You should learn to hide better."

"Aww," said Nico.

Nico texted *OMW* to the group chat. Calliope let him go in first, and before she stepped through
after him she knocked three times on the top of the doorway.

---

The door to the haven had been broken in with a ram. Claire could see the dents, and the splintering
round the handle and the lock.

"Our contact may be dying or already dead, so why don't we proceed?"

Inside, the place was a ruin. Wires hung where machines had been ripped out of the walls, the
computers with them. Filing cabinets had been opened, knocked over, gone through. And there were
two husks on the floor that had been vampires, their heads taken off, burned to a crisp. Touch
them, and they would fall to powder.

Kamal hesitated at the threshold. With no host, there was nobody to invite him in, and nothing
stopped him.

He looked at the husks. There was nothing in them for him. There was no coming back from this,
either. They were gone.

---

Kamal wanted blood, a trace of it, anything that might tell him who these two had been. Calliope
helped Nico look, and it was Nico who found it: a trace of vitae. Whose, nobody could say.

Kamal tasted it. Two Kindred. One strong. The other very strong, stronger than anything he had
ever tasted; very old, or very close to Caine. Baruch had been about as strong as the weaker.

Claire went to the filing cabinets. Whatever had happened, they did not tell her. But Baruch had
kept information on everybody, a great many Kindred, and not only in Quebec City, all of it in his
careful hand, most of it banal.

She found her own file, and Kamal's. Neither looked as though anyone had been through it.
Richart's and Calliope's lay on the floor nearby; if anything had been taken from them, it was not
the whole. Nico's was not there at all.

Calliope grabbed hers. Richart asked for his, politely, and Claire handed it over.

---

A hallway led off the main room. The lighting there was still on, and tasteful. They had been
here before, and knew that the first room off the hallway was for guests. The rest had always been
off limits.

Nico wandered off down it. He wanted to find out whether Baruch had kept a picture of him
somewhere else.

In the guest room there had been a struggle. Blood was flung across the walls and the carpet, as
if from slashing, not pouring. Nico stopped with his head at a strange angle, half on one foot,
and stared into the splatter.

Richart followed him some of the way, then went on to the next room. It had been opened, and gone
through, but not thoroughly: a storeroom, for things put away for a long time and taken out now
and then. He found a couple of curios in it that he could not identify, wrapped in newspaper, and
took them. He would find out later what they were.

Calliope went to the last room. Its door had been locked, and broken open. Inside was Baruch's
resting room, and it was nothing as crude as a coffin: a four-poster bed hung with curtains and
frills, heaped with duvets and pillows, that could be closed up around its sleeper. A wardrobe of
his exquisite suits. He had kept work and rest apart, and there had been no fighting here.

She searched it anyway. Whatever a person keeps closest, they keep near where they sleep. There was
a small meditation space in one corner: a low seat, a small carpet, some paraphernalia around them.
The only thing she could think of was something she had heard of and never seen, the path of
Golconda, the taming of the Beast.

---

Kamal went to the guest room, where he could smell blood, and found Nico there with his head on
one side.

"Nico, may I come in?"

Nico wiggled a hand at him. It could have meant anything. Kamal took it as a yes and went in.

Nico flopped onto the bed. "Did you just say something? Are you alright?" And then: "Yeah, having a
great time. I was just thinking about those blood splatters. They're really cool."

Kamal tasted this blood too. While he did it Nico explained. These two had been working on a
really phenomenal play together, and they had had a terribly significant disagreement about the
climax, and things had got very violent.

Kamal nodded respectfully, as if it were a reasonable idea.

A third Kindred. Stronger than the weaker of the other two, and weaker than the stronger. And the
oldest of them, and this one, were the most delicious vampire blood he had ever had. There was no
third body.

Kamal left Nico bouncing on the bed, and went to find someone who might be more help.

---

"Claire, the blood in this room is from a third Kindred. Also quite an old one. But I haven't seen
the body for it."

Claire went over to the two bodies and looked at them properly. On one of them something had
melted right into the remains. It was the material of a mask. She was quite certain now. One of
these was Baruch.

---

Calliope called out for Kamal, quietly, only for him. He came.

"I think I can help you," she said, and pointed at his wrist, where the stigmata were soaking his
sleeve. She held out her own. There were little bite marks already around it.

Kamal bowed his thanks, and bit.

Then she walked out of the room without another word.

---

Claire went back through the documents, for her own curiosity now. There was a whole section on
the Tremere of the city. The part on her own house was thin, and might have been gone through. But
there was a packet on the Tremere who had gone over to the Sabbat, and it was thick. She put it in
her messenger bag.

---

They came back together in the main room.

Nico's theory had grown. One of them had wanted to take the play into a musical, right at the
climax. The other had said no, absolutely no singing, and here they all were.

Claire pointed down at the melted mask. Baruch was probably one of the two.

"We don't even know that that's him," said Calliope. "It could be the mask. You could get those
anywhere."

Then she turned to Nico. "Why don't you have a file?"

"I don't know. I like it when people keep pictures of me."

He wanted to find it too.

"So we have two dead bodies," said Kamal, "three blood sources, all of which are more powerful than
any of ours. I suggest, if we don't have anything more productive to do here, we leave."

"Let's get a drink."

Baruch had been fastidious about his security. That he had died in his own haven was a great deal
to reckon with. Whoever had done this had come from inside, or had come with very good
intelligence, and had truly killed two powerful vampires. Baruch had known far more than any of
them had realised, and a great deal of it was gone now, in the hands of whoever had come.
